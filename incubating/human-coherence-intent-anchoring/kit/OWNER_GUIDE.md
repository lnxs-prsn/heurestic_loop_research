# Owner guide — staying in step with a project others keep changing

For the human in charge of a project that AI agents (and maybe other people)
work on. Plain language; the agent-facing rules are in `SPEC.md`.

## What this gives you

Agents keep their bearings by reading the project's files every session. You
can't do that, and you shouldn't have to. After a break you will have lost
the way back in, your own idea will have moved on in conversations
elsewhere, and agents will have changed things faster than you could follow.
This structure gives you a way back in that works the way people actually
remember, and it keeps everything that reaches you tied to one thing: **what
the project is for** (its *intent*).

## The one idea

Everything is measured against the intent. A project can be very busy and
still stand still. What matters is not how much moved, but how much the
intent moved forward. So every item put to you says:
- which **stage** of the project it touches;
- its **impact**: does it *change what the project is for*, *move it forward*,
  *change only the route*, or *not touch it at all*;
- **why**, in one plain line.

You see the biggest movers first. Things that don't touch the intent are
handled by agents under rules you approved, and never pushed at you.

## What you keep (all plain files)

- **Intent timeline:** what the project is for, as it was at the start, at
  checkpoints, and now. Agents reconstruct it from evidence; nothing counts
  as settled until you confirm it.
- **Story map:** the project's main stages from idea to done, each marked
  *grasped*, *partial* or *open*. Like a road trip's main stops: the intent
  is the destination. Agents draft it and keep it marked; you confirm it.
- **Inbox:** one place to drop anything about the project that happened
  elsewhere: other conversations, notes, documents. Dropping it there doesn't
  adopt it; it comes back to you at your next re-entry, already digested.
- **Re-entry briefs:** one dated note per re-entry, kept as a series.
- **Owner log:** one line each time you sit down with the project.

## Coming back after a gap (re-entry)

You start it by asking ("bring me back up to speed"), or an agent offers it
when it notices signs you may have lost the thread. It never forces it.

1. **Before you arrive**, an agent digests everything that changed into what
   it changes, why, and how it means to move the project.
2. **The frame.** You get a short view: *what* the project is for, *why*,
   *how* it was going about it, and *when* things happened. Then where the
   project stood against that intent when you left. This is a set of cues,
   not a lecture: you'll recall most of it.
3. **Recall and ask.** Ask anything ("why did we choose X?"). Answers come
   from the files, with sources. Go as deep as you like; stop when you have
   the thread.
4. **What's new.** New material is grouped into directions, biggest effect
   first, each described in your project's original terms: what it would
   keep, change or drop, and whether it changes *what the project is for* or
   only *the route*. Nothing is recommended yet.
5. **How they fit together.** You see which directions build on which,
   and whether any truly compete or only need ordering.
6. **Your intent, drafted.** The agent drafts what the project is for now,
   from the evidence, with sources. You confirm it or correct it; you are
   never asked to restate the project from memory.
7. **The verdict.** Has the intent changed significantly, and has the road
   from where the project stands to that intent been bent? If neither, you
   are done. If so, you decide each difference: update the intent, keep it,
   park it as an open question, or make it a **sub-project built on top**.

You leave knowing where you are, and the record knows too.

## Reflection

Whenever the project feels slow, ask for a reflection. It shows, for a period
you choose, how much work and decision-making actually moved the intent, and
which stages advanced or stalled.

## What agents promise you

- You are never asked about the project before you have the frame.
- You never receive raw changes.
- You are never shown a reconstructed intent as if you had confirmed it.
- You are never asked something your earlier answers already decide; they
  apply your answer and tell you which one.
- You are brought in only when the evidence shows the intent changed, the
  road bent, or a question the evidence can't settle.
- After a long gap, the first thing you see is the frame, not a question.
- Every item names what it is in words, not just an ID.
